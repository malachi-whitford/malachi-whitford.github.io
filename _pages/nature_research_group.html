Novel Approaches to Terrestrial Undergraduate Research in Ecology

The NATURE Research Group brings Clovis Community College students into the full research process, from the first field observation to a peer-reviewed publication.

Most community college research experiences focus on teaching methods. NATURE goes further: I treat undergraduates much like graduate students, with more guidance but the same high standard of personal accountability. Students design and run studies, analyze their own data, present at research symposia, and are expected to work toward publishing in peer-reviewed journals.

Current Projects

Species distribution modeling

Invasive Squamates in California

Using MaxEnt species distribution models, this group is identifying the habitat requirements of invasive lizards and snakes in California and projecting where they may spread under future climate scenarios.

Team: J. Chandler, J. Brennan

Species distribution modeling

California Pipevine and Climate Change

This group is modeling the habitat of California pipevine (Aristolochia californica) to test whether the apparent gap in its southern range is real or reflects undocumented populations, and whether climate change threatens those southern populations.

Team: C. Hernandez

Seed dispersal

Gray Foxes as Seed Dispersers

While deploying camera traps, we found gray fox (Urocyon cinereoargenteus) scat packed with coffeeberry (Frangula californica) seeds. This project tests how passing through a fox's gut affects coffeeberry germination and dispersal.

Team: V. Castillo, H. Cortez

Animal behavior

Pocket Mouse Predator-Deterrent Signals

Coarse-haired pocket mice (Chaetodipus sp.) have been seen approaching rattlesnakes and oscillating their hindquarters in what appears to be an antipredator display. Students are analyzing this behavior and the contexts in which it occurs. Future work will test how snakes respond and whether the display is an honest signal, like the displays of closely related kangaroo rats.

Team: V. Uy, G. Sprenger
